Lab Ready is a scenario-based onboarding course that turns a 70-rule chemical safety contract into seven decisions new lab technicians actually face: reading a label, checking a fume hood, knowing when to clean up a spill and when to call for help, and leaving a station safe for the next person. I designed it for adult learners and built and published it in Articulate Rise 360, with practice in every lesson and a job aid that stays at the bench.

The Problem

In college chemistry labs and in my own classroom, safety came the same way: pages of rules and dense bullet points, signed and filed away. I handed out that kind of contract to my own students, about 70 rules across two pages. People skim, sign and forget, and a list of rules doesn't tell you what to do when a spill is too big, a hood alarm goes off, or a senior coworker cuts a corner.

The Solution

I take complex information, like long regulations and pages of bullet points, and turn it into scenarios people can actually practice. Here, I rebuilt the rules around the moments new technicians face. Each lesson opens with why it matters on the job, teaches one decision, and gives practice right away: a fume hood to inspect, spills to clean up or call in, a waste label to fix. Experienced hires can test out, and everyone leaves with a one-page card for their station.

Learning objectives

What learners will be able to do

Given a real task at the bench, a new lab technician will be able to:

  1. 01

    Read a chemical label and Safety Data Sheet to name a chemical's hazards before using it.

    Measured by: the label and hazard questions in the final check.

  2. 02

    Check a fume hood and choose the right controls and protective gear for a task.

    Measured by: finding the problems in Hood Check, plus the glove question.

  3. 03

    Decide whether to clean up a spill or call for help.

    Mastery: the right call in all three Clean It or Call It? scenes.

  4. 04

    Respond to a splash, a fire or an unknown chemical in the first 60 seconds.

    Measured by: the fire and old ether bottle scenarios.

  5. 05

    Label and sort hazardous waste, and leave the station safe for the next person.

    Measured by: all six errors found on the waste label, plus the container sort.

Each objective maps to one of the seven decision moments and a section of the Lab Ready Card.

My thought process

How the design evolved

Lab Ready grew out of problems I've seen myself, as a chemistry student, in the lab, and in my own classroom. Here's the path, in order.

Step 01

Start in the lab

I've spent years in chemistry labs, first as a college chemistry student and then as a chemistry teacher. Safety was always handed over the same way: long documents, dense bullet points, and a signature at the end.

Step 02

Notice the gap

Knowing the rules on paper isn't the same as knowing what to do when a beaker tips or a hood alarm goes off. I saw that gap in my own classroom, so I started with the safety contract I gave my students.

Step 03

Teach it to adults

The same problem follows people into their first lab job. I asked how I'd teach what I taught my students to an adult new hire, someone who brings experience, wants to know why, and needs to use it right away.

Step 04

Read the real standards

Cal/OSHA §5191 and a university Chemical Hygiene Plan raised the bar and exposed errors: 15 vs. 20 minutes for an eye flush, the outdated term MSDS, and a contact lens rule the adult standard doesn't include.

Step 05

Turn notes into scenarios

I took pages of regulations and long bullet lists and boiled them down to 7 decisions, each one something learners can practice: a hood to inspect, a spill to clean up or call in, a waste label to fix.

Step 06

Make it work for real adults

I added a test-out path for experienced techs, a “why this matters” card in every lesson, audio and transcripts, and a 6-touchpoint journey. Then I built and published the course in Rise.

Practice in every lesson

I wireframed every lesson before opening Rise, so each block had a job before I built it. Each lesson reads like a short story: a photo with a caption, a few sentences, then practice. Each lesson teaches one decision and practices it right away.

The course is one of six touchpoints

Training doesn't end when the course does. I planned a journey from before day one to a refresher every three years: a welcome email with the lab's safety plan, a day-one walk-through, the course, a station card and weekly self-check, a supervisor check at 30 days, and a short refresher before any new chemical or procedure.

Accessibility & UDL

Designed so more learners can get in

Built in

  • Every hazard pictogram has a text label, so color is never the only cue.
  • Audio narration, a transcript and captions are available in every lesson.
  • Experienced hires can test out with a short self-check; everyone else follows a guided path.
  • No time limit on the final check, and a timer that can be turned off in timed practice.
  • Alt text on every graphic, and a click-to-place option for drag activities.

Next accessibility pass

  • Check every color pair in the Rise theme against WCAG 2.2 AA contrast.
  • Test the full course with a keyboard and a screen reader.
  • Rebuild Hood Check and Clean It or Call It? in Storyline with a logical keyboard focus order.
  • Publish the Lab Ready Card as an accessible PDF.

Lessons Learned

The biggest lesson was that the source material had problems of its own. The student contract and the adult standards disagreed on simple facts, like how long to flush an eye. Reading the actual regulation, not a summary of it, changed the course.

I also learned Rise by building the whole course in it, from the first block to the published link. Wireframing first meant every block had a job before I opened the editor. Where Rise's built-in blocks couldn't do what I planned for Storyline, I swapped in a labeled graphic and a scenario block, and I noted the Storyline upgrade as the next step.
